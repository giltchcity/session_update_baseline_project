A. Robust in-place model: posterior predictive with parameter uncertainty (replaces moment plug-in)
==============================================================================================
Present code (presentMoments/presentBeta): moments (m, v) of the object's looks, shrunk to the
population with kRobustLooks pseudo-looks, v >= population v0, variance floor 1e-4, concentration
c = m(1-m)/v - 1 >= 2, then a POINT Beta(m c, (1-m) c) is plugged in as the in-place density of the
next look's seen-through share f. Failure mode (e02a34d run, real A inst 7): a few looks with f ~ 0
give m ~ 0.001-0.01 and v at the floor 1e-4, c ~ 10-100, and a look at f = 0.38 scores llr 81.7.
The point Beta claims a precision the handful of looks cannot support.

Model (Gelman et al., Bayesian Data Analysis, 3rd ed., ch. 5, hierarchical model for proportions,
sec. 5.3 rat-tumour example; hyperprior p(alpha, beta) proportional to (alpha + beta)^(-5/2), eq. 5.9):
  - Look i of object j: share f_ij (one observation per look; samples of one look are strongly
    correlated, design effect ~180, so a look is not n independent Bernoulli draws).
  - f_ij | object j  ~ Beta(mu_j c, (1 - mu_j) c)       c: look-to-look concentration (shared)
  - mu_j             ~ Beta(m0 c0, (1 - m0) c0)          population of objects
  - (m0, c0), c      ~ hyperprior; in (mean, concentration) form p(m, c) prop. c^(-3/2)
                       (eq. 5.9 with the Jacobian of alpha = m c, beta = (1 - m) c).
Assumptions: shares exchangeable within an object and objects exchangeable within the population
(the same exchangeability the present shrinkage already assumes); f in (0, 1) (zero/one shares
enter with the Jeffreys continuity (k + 1/2)/(n + 1), the form already recorded for the bounds).
Prediction for the next look of object j:
  p(f* | data) = integral Beta(f*; mu_j c, (1 - mu_j) c) p(mu_j, c | f_j., population) dmu_j dc.
With few looks the posterior of c (and mu_j) is broad, so the predictive is a scale mixture of
Betas with heavy upper tail: f* = 0.38 after three looks at ~0 is surprising but not decisive.
With many looks the posterior concentrates and the predictive tends to the plug-in Beta: nothing
changes where the data support the current precision. No floor or concentration bound is needed
(the 1e-4 and the >= 2 become unnecessary; they stay only as numerical guards if ever reached).
Computation: a fixed grid over (mu, log c) per object (e.g. 64 x 64), likelihood from the stored
looks (<= 256 per object, already kept), population part as the prior on mu_j from the pooled
object means; evaluated once per scored look. Within-object looks keep "judge, then learn".
Expected effect on the failures: real A inst 7 at 243.8 s (three in-place looks before it) gets a
predictive wide enough that 0.38 is moderate; objects with hundreds of quiet looks keep their sharp
model, so moved objects (f 0.6-1.0) still score strongly.

B. Why the monitor's stray points (desk, ~0.09 m off) are "reliable"
==============================================================================================
reliableSample (projected_physical_evidence.cpp): a cell votes iff it was never seen through while
the object stood in place AND (state inherited OR never identified in place OR the cell model's
P(surface of this object | own/other label counts) >= 1/2). Mechanism for the stray points:
  1. They are part of the state's geometry: the monitor's reconstructed segment contains points
     outside the monitor (mask overflow onto the desk / the black laptop during reconstruction).
  2. The same overflowing masks give those cells own-identity hits in in-place frames, so the cell
     model counts them as the object's surface (own hits, few other-label observations).
  3. They were not seen through while in place in the frames that taught the record (at those
     views they project onto pixels that still carry the monitor label), so no veto.
  4. At 246 s a new viewpoint projects them onto desk pixels without the monitor label; depth is the
     desk ~0.09 m behind: seen-through verdicts, i.e. absence evidence from cells that never were the
     monitor.
Direction (not implemented): a cell's reliability should also require geometric consistency with
the object's surface when seen in place (on-surface readings of the cell itself, not only label
hits through a mask that may overflow); i.e. the cell model's evidence should be per-cell depth
agreement in in-place frames, not label co-occurrence alone.

C. Own-label pixels without depth as in-place evidence (chair body, 174-274 points per A-end frame)
==============================================================================================
Today a projected cell whose pixel has no valid depth is "no verdict" whatever its label. If the
pixel carries the object's own identity, the detector sees the object on that viewing ray: under
"present" this happens at the object's in-place rate of own-label-without-depth, under "absent"
only by mislabelling (the old site's pixels carry the background or another identity). As a second
share in eq. (7): g = (# cells with own label and no depth) / (# cells in view); present model of g
learned like the seen-through share (same hierarchical predictive, part A), absent model uniform
(the convention eq. (7) uses for f); one-sided (only g below the in-place level speaks for absence).
For the A-end chair (g ~ 0.13-0.20 in view, own label on the body) the channel gives presence
evidence that counters the silhouette-edge seen-through readings; at an emptied site g ~ 0.

of look_tables_20261006.jsonl (needs a FRAME_DIAG counter of own-label-no-depth cells).
